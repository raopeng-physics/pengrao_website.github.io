## The uprising

We, a long suffering and tormented people, have always lived in our town, this enormous expanse of low-lying brick houses and stone huts, interspersed with narrow muddy lanes and small empty openings. For generations we lived silently in our misery without protest, until there was finally proclaimed one day a call to arms:

'Citizens and comrades! Our long oppressed people have finally risen up against injustice and are now appealing to you urgently for your help! The situation is indeed critical. For long we have tenaciously endured our destitution, but in our hard lives we have each endured only by ourselves, whereas we now need to come together as a people; for the upcoming struggle the weapons we possess are few in number, and even those we have are too primitive to be of any use.

'But look around, comrades! Everywhere there is uproar in the streets, everywhere there is indignation at injustice! For we as a people have awakened! What we lack in materials, we will make up for by unity; what we lack in organisation, we will make up for by determination! 

'We therefore call upon you to go to the streets, go to the people and bring them outside. To show that not for one more day will we accept our poor, miserable fate. Not for one day longer will our people perish in silence.

'Long live the uprising! Long live the power of the people!'

Soon the pamphlets were flowing in every corner of the town, each one of us stepped out onto the streets as told and marched in unison. For days the entire town was consumed by turmoil, there was little one could do but feel the comradeship and excitement of those around us. We all expected something great yet terrible to come. However, as days progressed, things had remained the same, and some people started to doubt if the whole effort had been, after all, futile. The insurrection went on while gradually most people had grown tired; amidst the chaos it was impossible to maintain our daily activities, we were all starving and lacking in basic necessities. Until one day a storm broke out on a cold winter morning, and, completely exhausted, we all went back to our houses so as to carry on living. Throughout this time there was nothing from anyone else. No one had spoken of us or of our struggles. No one had done so. 

## Untitled 

As he lay in bed, tides, such as he had never felt before, rose from his chest and swept over him. As each wave came, the sounds and colors around him became weak and pale, like voices and photos of old acquaintances from very far, far away, those whom he barely recognised, finally arriving. And slowly each wave became stronger and more drawn out than the last, as if building up towards something incomprehensible, terrible and thrilling, until the waves coalesced into one single everlasting tide so tightly seamed together that it left him gasping for air, drowning out the mere human sense of hearing and seeing.  

Yet in the darkness he felt someone’s hand fall on his fingers, their palms pressed together. It gently pulled his hand away from him then squeezed his fingers just so a little harder. There was something of solace in the touch, something familiar from a past long forgotten, something serene, playful, almost cheerful, insignificant in its mundaneness and daily gaieties, yet so vast, foreign and all-encompassing that he felt the strangeness smothering him. This was the place that he had come from, where his childhood belonged, where one would again recover past happiness and all memories that were lost. It was a place that had called to him since his youth and to which he could not return forever.

But no one could hear the yearning of a dying man. Silence befell his thoughts and engulfed him. Then he was dead.
